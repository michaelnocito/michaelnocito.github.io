Analyst Prep Kit

What it is   Skill kits and how-to guides for the analyst tools
Why it is here   Evidence of explaining the work, not only doing it
Built with   HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and a lot of editing
Skill kits across Excel, SQL, Tableau, Power BI, Python and statistics, plus 46 standalone how-to guides. Written for people who are new to the work, because that is what I needed and could not find.

Why this belongs in an analysis portfolio

Half of an analyst's job is producing a number. The other half is explaining it to someone who will act on it and does not do this for a living.

The second half is harder to evidence in a portfolio. A dashboard shows I can build. It does not show whether I can make a finance manager understand why the denominator changed. This is what I have instead of taking that on faith.

The rules it is written under

Every page in it follows a written standard, which is itself the point. The ones that matter most:

RuleWhat it means in practice
Write one level downOne idea per sentence, about fifteen words. Terms defined where they are used, never assumed
The reader can always orientAt any point on the page: why am I here, what is the idea, what do I do now
Make the reader produce somethingThey attempt it before the answer appears. Reading an answer is not learning it
One directive, always visibleWhat to do right now is on screen while scrolling, including on a phone
Give the whole thing awayThe complete method, nothing held back for a paid version

What building it taught me that transfers

Most explanations fail at the vocabulary, not the concept. People do not get stuck on what a join does. They get stuck because nobody told them what a key is, and were too far in to ask.

An instruction that cannot be followed is not an instruction. Watching people work through these taught me the difference between "check the settings" and "click the cell showing 0.19%, then right-click it". The second is longer and it is the only one that works.

Naming the fork before the work. Before any step whose purpose is not obvious, say what it decides: the question, the possible answers, and what would follow from each. That habit came out of teaching and it made my analysis write-ups better.
